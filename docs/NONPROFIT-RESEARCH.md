# Nonprofit homepage research

Research note for the Flamivor Charlotte redesign. I reviewed the readable content and navigation hierarchy on each organization's official homepage. The sources below are text-extraction views, not pixel-level visual audits; I make no claims about their colors, typefaces, spacing, or exact image composition. Image observations are limited to accessible image labels exposed in the page text.

## Official homepage observations

- **[DonorsChoose](https://www.donorschoose.org/)** — Opens with a student-centered headline, a short explanation, and a direct project-browsing action. Search by topic/teacher/school and by place follows immediately, then the page exposes urgent requests and supply needs. Later sections explain how the organization works, transparency, and recurring giving. The visible image is only labeled “Image” in text extraction, so its subject/composition is unknown here. **Useful for Flamivor:** let a visitor act on a concrete thing (a guide or participation path) early, and keep local context easy to reach. A project marketplace and fundraising logic would be inappropriate without actual chapter offerings.

- **[Room to Read](https://www.roomtoread.org/)** — The homepage introduces its broad goal, then directs visitors into two clear work portfolios, with supporting resource destinations (“Discover She Creates Change” and “Read With Us”). A reach statement is followed by a “Latest” story list with titles, authors, topic/location labels, and read-more actions. The page text exposes no useful photo descriptions for evaluating image treatment. **Useful for Flamivor:** make the chapter’s actual current work and free resources distinct destinations; add news only when there are genuine updates to publish. Do not borrow global impact counts.

- **[Girls Who Code](https://girlswhocode.com/)** — The text-extracted page begins with a strategic goal and then gives program entry points by audience/age (Clubs and Pathways). It moves through an emerging-technology feature, organizational reach figures, donation action, a student quote, news, and partner information. Image labels include a community image, students, and a named person, but the text view does not expose their visual framing. The page says JavaScript is required, so its text order may not capture the complete rendered layout. **Useful for Flamivor:** audience labels can make different next steps understandable. For a small chapter, use learner / volunteer / partner actions only where each links to a real path; omit large-scale program claims and testimonial patterns unless verified and approved.

- **[Reading Partners](https://readingpartners.org/)** — The accessible content starts with a wide image sequence described through alt text (student with sign, volunteer/student high-five, tutoring scenes), then a concise literacy headline and a “Take action” link. “Programs,” impact/report links, and a location finder follow. A dedicated “Take Action” section separates volunteering, service, requesting a program, and donating. **Useful for Flamivor:** connect authentic learning imagery to a clear chapter statement, then separate actions by visitor intent. The image subjects are supported by the site’s alt labels; exact crop, color, and order are not verified visually. Do not adopt its impact figures or quote content.

- **[City Year](https://www.cityyear.org/)** — Its text hierarchy pairs a student-focused headline and short explanation with an image label of a corps member instructing a pupil. It then presents an “Education by the numbers” block, a narrative about change, stories, a student quote, and three distinct action cards: join, donate, and partner. The homepage also has a persistent-looking donate action and a top-level apply link in extracted navigation. **Useful for Flamivor:** distinguish the primary participation action from supporting paths, and use a real student-learning image when its provenance is clear. Skip statistics, application promises, donation asks, and quotes that the Charlotte chapter cannot substantiate.

- **[826 National](https://826national.org/)** — The page prioritizes student writing itself: a brief writing-led statement, a “I would like to read” control, filters, and a feed of student pieces with genre, title, student attribution, and a short excerpt. Later it explains its approach, shows network chapters, and offers join-the-movement actions. The text view confirms image elements alongside writing cards but does not describe their visual style. **Useful for Flamivor:** let real educational material be the proof: put an original guide or practical exercise in a prominent, browsable position, with a simple category/search control. Don’t invent student work or representative chapter stories.

## Practical direction for a new local chapter

For a chapter without established outcome metrics or a calendar of events, the strongest reusable sequence is: clear Charlotte identity and one-sentence purpose; one genuine, provenance-labeled learning image or original guide; three concise visitor paths for learners, volunteers, and partners; a short explanation of work being developed; actual leadership names; then the real join form. Keep free learning material accessible without sign-in. Use stories only when they are real and approved, and replace institutional impact blocks with specific, current facts about the chapter. The supplied phoenix logo and burgundy/cream colors remain fixed; black may be explored as a supporting visual color without recoloring or competing with the mark.

## Evidence limits

These observations reflect the homepage content available through text extraction on October 9, 2026. They are evidence of content hierarchy, action labels, and image alt text only. They do not establish visual palette, typography, layout geometry, motion, responsive behavior, or whether a script-dependent module appears in a different order after rendering. Treat these as content and information-architecture references, not as measured design specifications.
